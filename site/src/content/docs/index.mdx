## Why

Every tool that starts an agent on a piece of work meets the same four
failures. The agent starts before it can take a prompt, and the prompt
is lost. A pane goes quiet while a test runs, and someone takes the
quiet for done. A worktree outlives its work, or a branch goes with its
worktree while it still holds commits nobody landed. flume handles all
four once, for one place at a time, so your own `/launch`, millpond,
headwater, sounding and crew start work the same way.

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="One launch, one place" icon="code-branch">
    A launch names a repository, a kind, a brief and a name. flume makes
    a worktree on `<kind>/<name>` and makes it ready by the repository's
    own declaration of what a worktree needs.
  </Card>
  <Card title="A fresh agent for each stage" icon="rocket">
    Each stage starts a new agent in a herdr pane. flume sends the
    stage's prompt once the agent takes input, and confirms it in the
    agent's transcript.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Ended by its deliverable" icon="approve-check">
    A stage ends when its deliverable exists, or when its agent stops
    short and says what it needs. A quiet pane ends nothing.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Tidied when it is done" icon="seti:folder">
    Once the work lands or is dropped, flume removes the place it made.
    It keeps a branch that holds commits its target lacks.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## The name

In a watermill the flume is the channel that carries water from the
pond to the wheel, a measured flow to where it does work. In the family,
flume carries one item from millpond to one worktree, where an agent
does the work. sluice gates what lands, and the Flywheel keeps turning.

## Where it stands

flume is designed and not built. Its design lives in
[agentplot/blueprints](https://github.com/agentplot/blueprints) with
the design of every tool around it, and its statement comes next. The
[roadmap](/flume/roadmap/) says what gets built in what order.
