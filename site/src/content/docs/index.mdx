## Why

Every tool that starts an agent on a piece of work meets the same four
failures. The agent starts before it can take a prompt, and the prompt
is lost. A pane goes quiet while a test runs, and someone takes the
quiet for done. A worktree outlives its work, or a branch goes with its
worktree while it still holds commits nobody landed. flume handles all
four once, for one place at a time, so your own `/launch`, a script
and any tool that launches work all start it the same way.

## One queue for the host

Every launch on a host joins one queue, whoever launched it. A launch
names its kind, and may give its points, how much agent work its caller
judges it to be, and the stream it runs in. The host's configuration
declares its streams, each with its own bound on agents running, its
priority and the pool of accounts its agents run under, all beneath the
host's own bound. A stream may also bound its points in flight, or set
a pace, such as 8 points a day, so a side project moves at a steady
rate without taking every free agent. Points, the points bound and the
pace are decided and wait on their build
([roadmap](/flume/roadmap/#decided-not-built)).

A place's first stage starts only while the host, its stream and an
account of its pool have room, and its stream's points bound and pace
leave room for its points. Until then it waits, and status says what it
is short of. Waiting places start by their stream's priority, then
their own. flume takes every launch and refuses none for want of room.
[Agent settings](/flume/guides/agent-settings/#streams) says how to
declare streams and pools.

## One place, whole

Once its first stage starts, a place runs its kind through on its own.
Each stage has a fresh agent, and the next starts once its deliverable
exists. Then the merge queue lands the work, and flume tidies the place.

## The name

In a watermill the flume is the channel that carries water from the
pond to the wheel, a measured flow to where it does work. flume carries
one piece of work to one worktree, where an agent does it, and sluice
gates what lands.

## Where it stands

flume runs. A fix, a chore and a change are carried from their launch to
their landing through sluice, and their places tidied, with nobody's
word between the stages. Under herdr a watch keeps every place moving,
and each end reaches whoever waits on it. The host's queue runs its
streams by their bounds and priorities, on pools of accounts; a place's
points, and a stream's points bound and pace, are decided and wait on
their build. Releases for Linux and macOS
are on [GitHub](https://github.com/agentplot/flume/releases), and
[Install and set up](/flume/install/) says how to start. The
[roadmap](/flume/roadmap/) says what is decided and not yet built.
