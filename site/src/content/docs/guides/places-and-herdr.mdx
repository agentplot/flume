A place is the worktree flume makes for one launch. Every stage of the
work runs there, one agent at a time, and flume removes it once the
work lands or is dropped. Where the agents run is the host's choice,
and each stream's: in herdr panes, or as commands flume prints.
`flume open` shows a stream's agents in a window.

## The place

flume makes a plain `git worktree` of the repository, named
`<kind>--<name>`, on the branch `<prefix>/<name>`, where the prefix is
the kind's. It cuts the branch from the base's
current revision. Where the repository's sluice policy lands the base
on a remote, flume fetches that remote first and cuts the branch from
the remote's revision. Where the policy lands the base locally, it cuts
from the local base, where each landing goes. Where no merge queue is
bound, or the policy names no table for the base, flume fetches the
base from `origin` and cuts from `origin/<base>`, so a local copy that
lags origin is never read. It cuts from the local base only where the
repository has no `origin`, or `origin` holds no such branch. A launch
whose fetch fails otherwise is refused. `flume status` says beside each
place's base where its revision was read.

The repository is the directory git finds one in, a clone's main
worktree, or, in a bare layout, `project/project.git` with its
worktrees beside it, the directory `project`. Every place goes under
flume's own data directory, inside no working tree or directory of the
repository, so the repository's status, its formatters and its other
tools never see one: `$XDG_DATA_HOME/flume/places/<id>/<kind>--<name>`,
by default `~/.local/share/flume/places/<id>/`, where `<id>` is the
repository's, the same id its agents' identities carry. `flume status`
names each place's location. A place an earlier flume made at
`<repository>/places/<kind>--<name>` stays there until it is tidied,
and is named by its kind and name as before.

A repository binds the place tool in its `.config/flume.toml`:

```toml
[profiles]
place = "git-worktree"
```

`git-worktree` is the one place profile, and a repository that names
none gets it. flume reads the `[profiles]` table at the default branch,
and never guesses a tool from another tool's files.

A launch is refused whole when anything of it already stands: the
place's directory, its branch, or a live place of the same kind and
name. Nothing is left behind by a refused launch.

## Making a place ready

A place is made ready by its merge queue, as the merge queue makes
ready the working copies of its own runs. flume runs no step of its
own. With sluice bound, flume runs one command in the new place:

```sh
sluice equip <base> --at <revision> --json
```

`<revision>` is the base's revision the launch read, which the place's
branch was cut from. sluice reads `.config/sluice.toml` at that
revision and runs in the place what it runs before a run of its own:
the `[tests] toolchain` the policy names, then, with
`[tests] runner = "moon"`, `proto install` of what the place's
`.prototools` pins, `moon --version` and `moon setup`. It answers each
step with whether it passed and what it printed, and stops at the first
that fails. Those steps are the place's readiness. flume keeps nothing
a step makes.

`sluice equip` runs with the host user's login environment, nothing of
the caller's session, so the sluice it runs is the one that
environment's `PATH` finds. sluice 0.67.0 is the first with
`sluice equip`. An older one fails the readiness at its one step,
`sluice equip`, with sluice's words, as does a refusal or an answer
flume cannot read.

A step that fails leaves the place made: a fix or a chore starts anyway
and its prompt names the failed step, and a change waits with the step
in `flume status`. A retry of that first stage runs `sluice equip` again
at the launch's revision, so sluice reads the policy as the launch read
it and installs what the place holds then, its agent's commits among
them. A repository whose merge queue equips it with no step, or that
binds none, is ready at once, and the prompt says it declares no
readiness.

`flume status` gives each step in one line, passed or failed, and under
a failed step the last ten lines it printed, its terminal escapes
stripped:

```console
$ flume status change--tabs-in-quotes
~/.local/share/flume/places/f5f5e14159a8/change--tabs-in-quotes: change@2 (flume), live
  change--tabs-in-quotes · change@2 · construct · not ready
  branch change/tabs-in-quotes from main, head 84130b3cd2adae9a0ac2117884849d513da80181
  …
  readiness toolchain: passed
  readiness proto install: passed
  readiness moon --version: passed
  readiness moon setup: failed
    Installing node 22.11.0
    Error: node 22.11.0 could not be downloaded: the connection timed out
  stage construct: waiting_on_readiness, started 0 times
  …
  waits on its caller to retry construct (unready) since 1793287734
```

`flume status --json` gives what each step printed, its escapes and
all, passed or failed.

## The multiplexer: printed or herdr

The host's configuration names the multiplexer every stream's agents
run in, and a stream may name its own over it:

```toml
# ~/.config/flume/config.toml
[host]
multiplexer = "herdr"
```

`terminal = "herdr"`, the word written before `multiplexer`, reads the
same. With none, or `multiplexer = "printed"`, flume prints each agent's
command, `cd` into the place and the program with its environment and
prompt, for you to run. It can send nothing into a pane it does not
hold, so it cannot deliver a `tell` or a notice, or keep an agent open
for its answer. Ends stay pending
until `flume wait` or `flume inbox` collects them, and the place moves
on at each command of flume's that acts, or at a caller's watch
([keeping watch over the host](#keeping-watch-over-the-host)).

Under herdr 0.9.5 or later, flume opens each agent in a pane itself,
sends its prompts, reads its state, and delivers ends to the panes of
the recipients.

### Trying a multiplexer on one stream

A stream's `multiplexer` stands over the host's, so you can try a
setup on one stream while the others run as they did:

```toml
[host]
multiplexer = "herdr"

[streams.trial]
multiplexer = "printed"
```

Each start reads its stream's multiplexer and records it, and its agent
stays in the one it opened in: flume sends to it, reads it and ends it
there. Change a stream's multiplexer while a place runs and its live
agent stays where it is, its next stage opening in the new one; flume
tidies that place in both. A recipient's notice goes to the multiplexer
that holds a session of the recipient's name. Status gives each agent's
multiplexer and each stream's, with where each came from.

The local layer, `config.local.toml`, may set a stream's
`multiplexer`, so you can try one on your own host without changing the
managed file. A stream whose multiplexer names no profile flume carries
refuses its own launches and starts, naming the name and the profiles
there are, and every other stream runs. No multiplexer decides whether
a command settles every place first: the host's watch does
([keeping watch over the host](#keeping-watch-over-the-host)).

## Places under herdr

<Diagram name="herdr" caption="A tell waits while its agent works and reaches it once the agent waits. retry with your words hands an answer to the agent kept open for it, which carries its stage on; stop ends an agent that went the wrong way, and a retry starts its stage again with a fresh agent." />

flume keeps each stream's agents in herdr sessions of the stream's.
Its own is the one its `session` names, else the one named by the
stream's name, and every agent of the stream opens there but those of
a kind or a stage the stream names a session of its own for
([a session for a kind or a stage](#a-session-for-a-kind-or-a-stage)).
So `flume open work`, or `herdr --session work`, shows the `work`
stream's agents in its own session ([seeing a stream's agents](#seeing-a-streams-agents)). The
`default` stream's agents run in flume's own session, `flume`, unless
`[streams.default]` names another. A launch's `--agent
session=<name>` opens its place's agents in that session whatever their
stream ([a stream's sessions](/flume/guides/agent-settings/#a-streams-sessions)).
flume starts a session's server, headless, the first time an agent
opens in it. Where herdr does not take a session's name, the start is
refused in herdr's words. In each session:

- **A workspace per repository and base**, labelled
  `<repository> <base>` from the repository's directory name, such as
  `acme main`. flume opens it the first time a place on that base
  starts in that session, so a base whose places run in two streams
  has a workspace in each.
- **A tab per place**, `<kind>--<name>`, in its base's workspace.
- **A pane per agent** in the place's tab. flume starts each agent with
  herdr's `agent start`, with the agent's environment set on the pane,
  so nothing is typed into a shell. The pane's shell reads no startup
  file, so nothing in your shell's files reorders the pane's `PATH`.
  flume starts the agent once that shell has come to its prompt, which
  on a loaded host can be some seconds after the pane opens. A start
  whose shell and agent aren't both up within two minutes ends never
  begun, with the pane's screen in `flume status`.
  The command that opened the agent, a launch, a retry or a settle,
  sends it its prompt once the agent has said it started and waits. The
  settles that follow read whether it was received. Until that
  command's open is done, nothing else is sent to the agent.

Each agent carries a herdr name made from its identity, at most 32
characters and ending in a digest of it. flume holds a pane by its id
and server, and finds it again by that name when the id no longer
answers, as after you move the pane to another workspace.

flume answers one question on an agent's behalf: whether it may work
in the place's folder, which Claude Code asks in a folder new to it.
A permission prompt, a sign-in, a first run's setup and a repository's
MCP servers' approval are left for a person. While the agent shows
one, its start stays starting and `flume status` says the place waits
on a person to answer the agent's dialog, with the dialog's words,
for as long as it shows. Once you answer it in the pane, the agent
starts and takes its prompt. flume also
checks the program in the pane's foreground. When it is not the one
the settings name, such as a `claude` your shell found earlier on the
`PATH`, flume closes the pane and refuses the start, naming what ran.

An agent's `beside` setting opens its pane as a split beside another
pane instead, named by its label or id on flume's server, or as
`herdr:<pane>@<socket>`, in whatever session that pane is. A
conversation stage uses it to open beside the caller's own pane.

Every herdr call flume makes runs under a bound, ten seconds where the
call states none, with the calling session's `HERDR_*` variables
stripped and the recorded server's socket passed. A call that does not
answer in time reads as unknown, never as an answer.

### An agent kept open

An agent that stops short needing an answer stays in its pane, kept
open for the answer for its `keep_open`, an hour by default ([the agent
kept open](/flume/guides/callers-and-holds/#the-agent-kept-open)). A
retry with words, or a tell, to its place is typed into that pane as a
tell is: once the agent waits, its input cleared first, read back as
received or typed once more. The agent reads the question and the
answer and carries its stage on in the same session, with everything
it had read. Once its time passes, it stops or its account is spent, a
settle lets it go and closes its pane once it is quiet, never forced.
An agent herdr brought back in a conversation's pane after a restart is
left as it stands.

Under the printed command flume can type nothing into the session you
ran, so it keeps no agent open. A stage that stops short waits on a
retry, which starts a fresh agent with the question and the answer once
the session you ran has ended.

### A session for a kind or a stage

A stream may open one kind's agents, or one stage's, in a session of
their own beside the stream's, so you find them apart from its other
work:

```toml
[streams.work.kinds.research]
session = "work-design"

[streams.work.kinds.release.stages.review]
session = "work-review"
```

Here a research place's agents open in herdr's session `work-design`, a
release's review agent in `work-review`, and its other agents and every
other place's in `work`. Each such session is a server of its own,
started headless the first time an agent opens in it, and laid out as
every session is: a workspace per repository and base, a tab per place.
A release place has a tab in `work` and one in `work-review`. Its review
agent ends with its stage, and its tidy closes its tab in each session,
and each base's workspace once it holds no tab of a live place and
nothing flume did not make.

The place keeps its stream wherever its agents open: they count in
`work`'s bound and the host's, take accounts from `work`'s pool and take
their label's share. [A stream's sessions](/flume/guides/agent-settings/#a-streams-sessions)
gives the order an agent's session is read in, and what refuses a
start.

## Seeing a stream's agents

`flume open <session>` shows a stream's agents one session at a time,
by the session's name, and `flume open` the `default` stream's own
session. What you open is a session, never a stream. A stream's own
session bears the stream's name where the stream names none, so you
open it by that name. A stream whose `session` names its own, as
`session = "desk"`, is opened by that name, `flume open desk`. A session
a stream names for a kind or a stage is opened by its name too. flume
shows it in its stream's view, else the host's:

```toml
[host]
multiplexer = "herdr"
terminal_app = "ghostty"

[streams.work]
title = "Work Agents"

[streams.work.kinds.research]
session = "work-design"

[streams.office]
terminal_app = "terminal"
title = "Office Agents"
```

Here `flume open work` opens a Ghostty window titled `Work Agents`
running `herdr session attach work`, `flume open work-design` one
titled `work-design` running `herdr session attach work-design`, and
`flume open office` a Terminal.app window titled `Office Agents` on the
session `office`.
Under herdr, flume names the herdr it runs by its full path, so the
window runs the same release whatever its own `PATH`. A session not yet
running is started headless first, before any of its places has an
agent, and stays running as every session's server does.

A name no stream's word gives is refused before the multiplexer or a
window is asked anything, naming each stream's sessions, so a mistyped
name starts no stray session:

```console
$ flume open work-desing
refused: no stream's word gives a session work-desing; each stream's sessions: the stream default gives the terminal's own, opened by no name; the stream office gives office; the stream work gives work and work-design
```

A stream's name that is not one of its sessions' is refused the same
way, naming the session it does give. A session two streams' words
give, as two streams naming one `session`, is refused naming both, and
a launch's session, which no stream's word gives, is not opened.

A stream's `view` is `terminal` unless the stream or the host names
`app`. The view `terminal` is a window of a terminal app running the
multiplexer's attach. The view `app` is the multiplexer's own app,
which titles its own window and needs no terminal app. herdr has no app
of its own: the app of the same name runs on herdr-core, a backend with
sessions of its own. So a stream under herdr whose view is `app`
refuses its open, saying so.

### The terminal apps

- **`ghostty`**, Ghostty. On macOS flume runs `open -na Ghostty.app`
  with the window's title and the command, waiting up to ten seconds
  for it. Elsewhere it starts `ghostty --title=<title> -e <command>`
  apart from itself, under your login environment, and does not wait
  on it. Ghostty keeps the title it was given whatever the program
  inside sets. An `open` that cannot find Ghostty refuses in its words.
  Checked against Ghostty 1.4.0.
- **`terminal`**, Terminal.app, on macOS alone. flume asks Terminal
  through `osascript` to run the command in a new window and give its
  tab a custom title, waiting up to ten seconds. The first time, macOS
  asks you to let the program running flume control Terminal; an open
  you have not allowed is refused in `osascript`'s words, and the next
  opens once you allow it. Terminal shows the custom title beside what
  your Terminal profile adds there, such as the process or the window's
  size. It types the command into the window's shell, so a variable the
  command needs shows its value there. Checked against Terminal 2.15 on
  macOS 27.
- **`printed`**, where neither the stream nor the host names a terminal
  app. flume opens no window and prints the command on the answer's
  last line, after `run: `:

  ```console
  $ flume open work
  the terminal app printed opens no window; run this line to show herdr's session work (the stream's name) titled "Work Agents" (the stream's title):
    stream work, multiplexer herdr (the host's), view terminal (the default), terminal app printed (the default)
    its command /usr/local/bin/herdr session attach work
  run: /usr/local/bin/herdr session attach work
  ```

  You run the line, or a launcher of your own takes what follows
  `run: ` and runs it. A variable the command needs prints as
  `<name>=<set>`, never its value.

### Filing a window by its title

A window on a stream's own session carries its stream's `title`, else
the stream's name. A window on a session the stream names for a kind or
a stage carries the session's name, so it files apart from the
stream's own. A window manager that places windows by a rule on their
title files each window where you keep it. Match the stream's title within the
whole title, since Terminal.app may add to it.

flume keeps nothing of a window. Each asking opens a window of its own,
so a key bound to `flume open work` opens one each press; finding the
one already open is your window manager's. flume opens a window only
at your asking, never at a launch, a settle or a start, and never
raises or closes one.

## The herdr plugin

flume's herdr plugin shows flume's places in herdr: a popup of every
place, and the rows flume gives the sidebar. It keeps no process and
runs no settle. Install it at the tag of the flume you run, so the
plugin and the binary are one version:

```sh
herdr plugin install agentplot/flume/plugins/herdr --ref v<version>
```

It needs `flume` and `jq` on the `PATH` herdr's server runs with, and
herdr as the host's multiplexer or a stream's. It brings:

- **Nothing between herdr's start and your asking.** No process of the
  plugin's runs, no `flume settle` and no `flume status` on a timer.
  Places move when their agents go quiet, at each settle of a caller
  keeping watch over the host, and at each command of flume's that acts
  ([keeping watch over the host](#keeping-watch-over-the-host)).
- **A startup** that herdr runs once for each server as it starts. It
  reads herdr's configuration file, writes down what that file lacks
  for the plugin, which `flume status` gives while that server runs,
  and exits. Where an earlier release of the plugin left its watch
  running on that server, the startup ends it.
- **Display clues**, which flume shows at each settle that changes one:
  each agent's pane titled `<stage> · <state>` and named
  `<kind>--<name>`, and each base's workspace given the token `flume`, a
  count of its places: `1 running · 0 asking · 1 waiting on caller · 0
  stalled`. They show what status says, and nothing flume decides reads
  them back.
- **A sidebar row** on each agent's entry, the `$flume` token: the
  short form of its place's line, less the place's name and kind, which
  the entry already shows. That is the stage with a repeating stage's
  group, how many times it started, the change's tasks ticked and the
  place's state, as in `code 1/2 · run 1 · tasks 2/5 · working`. It is
  one of flume's clues, shown at the settles that change it.
- **A popup** of every place in flight, `agentplot.flume.places`. It
  reads `flume status --json` when you open it and again after each key
  that acts, and never on a timer, and shows each place's number and its
  line, the words its agents' status line gives, with its location
  beneath:

  ```text
  ▶ 1  fix--the-parser-reads-tabs · fix@1 · code · run 1 · working
       ~/.local/share/flume/places/f5f5e14159a8/fix--the-parser-reads-tabs
  ```

  The chosen place is marked `▶`. Each key acts at once, with no Enter:
  the arrow keys or `j` and `k` move the choice, and `1` to `9` choose
  the place so numbered. `o` opens its tab, `t` tells its agent the
  words you give, `s` stops its agent and `r` retries its stage; the
  words, and whether to force, are asked for as a line, ended by Enter.
  `q` or Escape closes the popup. Each key runs one flume command or
  focuses the tab, and none types into an agent's pane.

The rows and the popup work out nothing of their own: flume's settles
show the rows, and the popup copies the lines status gives.

### Keeping watch over the host

The plugin keeps no watch and decides nothing of how flume runs. A
caller keeps watch over the host by running

```sh
flume settle --every <time>
```

on that bound, from a timer of the host's or whatever else keeps time
there. Each run settles every place, an agent in it or none, and steps
each open submission's base, so a place whose submission waits on the
merge queue moves on within the bound. While the newest such settle
began less than twice its bound ago, flume runs unattended: its
commands leave the settling to the watch. While none does, flume runs
attended: every command of flume's that acts settles every place first.
The places then move at your commands and at their agents' quiet, and
what no agent's quiet reports, an agent killed with no word or a
submission the merge queue decided, waits for your next command that
acts. `flume status` says which under the host's part:

```console
$ flume status
…
  a caller keeps watch: every place settles every 2m, last at 1793287734
```

### Setting up herdr

The plugin changes none of herdr's configuration. Add the popup's key
and the sidebar's rows yourself, to the file herdr reads, or to
whatever writes it: the file `HERDR_CONFIG_PATH` names, else
`$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/herdr/config.toml`, else
`~/.config/herdr/config.toml`.

**The popup's key.** herdr's plugins declare no keys, so a key reaches
the popup only through an entry of your own. Add this one, or bind
another key the same way:

```toml
[[keys.command]]
key = "prefix+f"
type = "plugin_action"
command = "agentplot.flume.places"
description = "flume: places in flight"
```

`prefix+f` is free among herdr's own keys.

**The sidebar's rows.** The rows below are herdr's own plus flume's:

```toml
[ui.sidebar.agents]
rows = [
  ["state_icon", "machine", "workspace", "tab"],
  ["agent"],
  ["$flume"],
]

[ui.sidebar.spaces]
rows = [
  ["state_icon", "workspace", "$flume"],
  ["branch", "git_status"],
]
```

If you have rows of your own, add only the `$flume` entries. The pane
titles show wherever herdr shows a pane; the rows show only where your
sidebar names `$flume`.

**What `flume status` says is missing.** Until herdr's file holds the
entry and the rows, `flume status`, of one place or of every place,
names what the file lacks under the line naming the host's watch, in
words you can act on:

```console
$ flume status
…
  no caller keeps watch: every place settles at each command of flume's that acts
    herdr's configuration, ~/.config/herdr/config.toml, binds no key to flume's places: add a [[keys.command]] entry with key = "prefix+f", type = "plugin_action", command = "agentplot.flume.places", description = "flume: places in flight", as the README of flume's herdr plugin gives it
```

`flume status --json` gives the same words in the host's
`terminal_setup`. flume says them only when asked: no notice, no herdr
notification. The startup reads the file by its lines and parses no
TOML, so an entry written as an inline table may read as missing.
Nothing flume decides reads the words. herdr runs the startup as its
server starts, so a change to the file shows in `flume status` once
herdr starts again.

The plugin keeps what herdr's configuration lacks, `setup.json`, with
its server's socket, and the startup's log, `startup.log`, under
`$XDG_STATE_HOME/herdr/plugins/agentplot.flume`, one directory per
herdr server. flume gives a server's words while the socket it names
is present.

## Tidying

<Diagram name="tidy" caption="flume removes the landed fix's place and branch, removes the dropped change's place and keeps its branch, and leaves a worktree made by hand alone." />

A settle tidies a place once its work is over, landed, dropped or done,
and its agent has stopped. `flume tidy` does the same at once, for one
place or all. flume:

1. runs the teardown the merge queue's policy gives, `sluice release`
   for sluice, in the place;
2. removes the worktree with `git worktree remove`, never forced, so a
   place holding uncommitted or unignored work, or a lock, stays and
   flume names what holds it;
3. removes the branch only when `git cherry` against the base finds no
   commit the base lacks, judged against the remote's copy of the base
   where it lands on one. A branch with commits not landed stays, and
   flume names it once:

   ```text
   removed ~/.local/share/flume/places/f5f5e14159a8/chore--tidy-the-readme
   kept the branch chore/tidy-the-readme of ~/.local/share/flume/places/f5f5e14159a8/chore--tidy-the-readme, 1 commits not landed
   ```

4. closes the place's tab in each multiplexer its agents opened in,
   under herdr in each session it has a tab in. It closes its base's
   workspace there once that holds no tab of a live place and no pane
   flume did not open. A workspace where you opened a pane of your own
   stays open, and status names it under the host's `terminal_kept`,
   or that multiplexer's `kept` in `terminals`.

flume removes nothing it did not make: no worktree made by hand, no
repository's main worktree, and no herdr workspace, tab or pane of
yours. Its record of the place goes last, kept apart only while an end
of it is still pending for a recipient.
