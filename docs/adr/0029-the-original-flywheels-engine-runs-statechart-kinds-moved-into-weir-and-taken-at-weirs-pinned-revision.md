## Context and problem statement

Record 0028 brings statechart kinds beside simple kinds, and ruling T2 runs them on the original Flywheel's engine. flywheel-engine is the generic half of the original. It loads machine definitions, evaluates guards over a snapshot of evidence, plans a tick (`plan_tick`, pure, writing nothing) and applies it to one object (`apply`), and derives decisions from the states that carry one. It holds no store and does no IO: evidence comes through an `EvidenceSource` its caller serves, and an effect is a name with arguments its caller performs, planned only while its proof is absent (model §2.1 to §2.5). It names nothing of Flywheel's domain, which a test of its own holds. What it runs, the stage, session and place templates and the atoms they name, is data outside it.

The crate lives in agentplot/flywheel-next, a private repository with no licence file, in a workspace of twelve crates, nine of which take it by path. flume is public under MIT, and its kind format is weir's, read by weir-kinds at a revision of weir's main (core.4, core.22). The engine's `defs.rs` is the statechart's schema as serde types: the fields a statechart kind declares. How does flume take the crate?

## Considered options

1. A git dependency on agentplot/flywheel-next pinned by revision, as flume takes weir-kinds from weir today.
2. The crate moved into weir as a shared crate beside weir-kinds, and taken by flume at the revision of weir it pins for weir-kinds.
3. The crate copied into flume's tree as a crate of flume's own.

## Decision outcome

Option 2.

1. **The engine.** flywheel-engine runs statechart kinds (ruling T2). flume ticks a statechart kind's machine with it and decides no transition of its own: in each region the first transition whose guard holds fires, and an effect is performed only while its proof is absent (model §2.1, §2.3). Simple kinds run as today, by flume's own code (record 0028).
2. **Moved into weir.** The crate moves into agentplot/weir as a crate of weir's beside weir-kinds, its name the move's, with its tests, its toy machine and its test that it names no reader's domain. What is the original's own stays with the original: its loader of a directory of YAML files, since a statechart kind is written in weir's kind format and read by weir-kinds (ruling T3; weir C.4), and its recutils reader, which weir-capture already carries. The machine's types become the kind format's, so weir-kinds reads a statechart kind's declaration into the engine's own `Machine` and nothing defines a statechart's fields twice (record 0004; core.4).
3. **Taken at weir's pinned revision.** flume takes the engine as it takes weir-kinds: a git dependency on weir pinned to one revision of weir's main in the workspace's `Cargo.toml`, both crates from that one revision, moved together by a chore when weir changes (core.22). The format a kind is read in and the tick that runs it never come from two revisions.
4. **The original keeps its copy.** agentplot/flywheel-next is not changed for the move. Its crates go on taking their copy by path, at the revision the move was made from, and fixes to the engine are made in weir.

**How the engine meets flume.** The engine's shape is already flume's, which is why it is taken whole. A tick is pure and plans over one snapshot, so a settle reads the evidence once, at one head of the place's branch (25), and ticks once. Nothing runs between flume's commands (120, I8; core.13): the original's sweep every 60 seconds is herdr's watch running `flume settle` (122). An object's configuration, the times its regions were entered, its record, its counters and the responses it applied are part of the place's held state, written by compare-and-set (core.16). An effect's proof keeps a settle repeated from doing anything twice (74; core.17), and a response applied is never applied again. Evidence and effects are the templates' atoms, which flume serves and performs through its seams, one interface each (core.2), so the engine names no tool (core.1). The original's lease, a host's hold on an object, has no part in flume, where one host holds its places, and numbering decisions for a rail is the caller's. How each part fits is the build's (ledger item T1.c).

**Why not the others.** Option 1 asks flume's public build to fetch a private repository that carries no licence file. CI's runners would need a credential to clone it, nobody without one could build flume, and the only way out, making flywheel-next public, publishes the whole of the original to reach one crate. It also leaves the statechart's fields defined in flywheel-engine's `defs.rs`, outside weir, so the kind format has two definitions, weir-kinds' TOML for simple kinds and the engine's serde types for statecharts, against record 0004 and core.4; and two pins, weir's and flywheel-next's, must move in step for a kind's format and its tick to agree. It brings the YAML loader and `serde_yaml` 0.9.34, which its author marked deprecated. What it saves, a move and a second home for the crate, is small beside that. Option 3 defines the statechart's fields in flume, which core.4 rules out, makes a copy that drifts from the original's and from every later reader's, and leaves sounding and Flywheel's later runner no crate that reads the same kinds the same way.

**What option 2 costs.** The move is a change in weir: weir's gate, lints and conformance pack hold the engine, and weir carries and keeps a crate it did not write. weir's statement grows past the forms the tools meet in to say how a statechart kind runs, its first definition of a behaviour; weir still has no process and no state, since the tick is pure and the engine holds no store. The original's copy and weir's drift apart, which costs nothing while the original is not developed. And the move publishes the engine's source under weir's MIT licence: the licence the original's own manifest already declares for it, and a publication that linking the engine into flume, a public tool, makes in any case.

### Consequences

- weir's statement gains, in a change the user rules with the move (core.19): a section of Part A for the engine, saying the tick over one snapshot, the guard algebra, regions and submachines, effects planned only while their proof is absent, and decisions derived from the states that carry one; the line that the engine names no reader's object or atom and performs nothing itself (model §2.5); in B.4, a tick beside read-kinds, which given a statechart kind's machine and a snapshot of its evidence gives the transitions that fire with the effects to perform, the same snapshot always giving the same answer and reading changing nothing; the pack's cases for the tick, the original's toy machine among them; clauses after 93 and scenarios after S18.
- flume's statement gains (ledger item T1.a, which covers the engine): a clause after 174 that a statechart kind runs on weir's engine, each settle ticking it once over evidence read at one head (25), flume deciding no transition and performing an effect only while its proof is absent; in 84 and 85, read-kinds giving a statechart kind's machine with its kinds; a contract of Part B binding each atom the stage, session and place templates name to one seam's interface (105, 106); C.2 naming the engine's crate and the revision of weir it is read at; and, with the build (T1.c), what status reports of a statechart kind: where it is, its finals and the decisions inside it.
- Rule core.42 holds it, and core.4's details name the engine's types as the format's.
- flume's `Cargo.toml` gains the engine's crate from weir, at the revision weir-kinds is taken at, in the build after crew's rewrite onto flume (record 0028). Nothing is built for this record.
- flume's own code that runs a simple kind stays, `crates/flume-core`'s stages and `crates/flume-seams/src/held.rs`'s `StageState`, and the engine runs no simple kind (record 0028).
