## Context and problem statement

A stage's agent that cannot go on runs `flume needs`, and its stage ends short (41). The end goes to the place's recipients, and the place waits on its caller's retry (42, 43, 130). On deneb on 2026-11-05, 34 of 37 places had no recipient at all. A launch from a shell with no terminal session takes none (7), and the host's orchestrator launches that way, so its places' ends went to no one. The only reader was the orchestrator's own script, polling `flume status`. While the script was not running, overnight from 2026-11-04 to 11-05, two changes waited about nine hours on questions nobody saw.

The runner's places name the address `madswan/planner`. Clause 132 gives an address as `<partition>/<role>`, and no clause says what a partition is: flume reads the address as a key of the host's `[roles]` table, names the run from the part after the slash, and reads the part before it for nothing, beside the streams that already divide a host's work (143). crew's main-level roles, the design agent, the planner, ops, the reviewer and the operator, all stand as Claude Code sessions in herdr panes, outside flume, so nothing runs at that address and seven ends of one place stand pending (132). crew designed and built each role as a Flume kind at its own address (`roles-run-as-flume-kinds`), and has moved none onto it. flume reads a role's run from the host's own configuration, one `[roles]` entry per address, so a host of crew's would repeat every partition's every role, though crew already derives every fact of each run from its partitions into its own role record. crew had the runner launch and end role runs, since flume names a role's run by the role alone, so two partitions' planners in one repository would be one place, and since a run flume launches never ends of itself. Every place crew launches names the planner, though a question may be the design agent's or ops's.

A place launched from a herdr pane has its ends delivered into that pane. A restarted agent there gets a one-line notice about a place it never saw, and flume's plugin does nothing at the start of a session flume did not start. Nothing bounds how long a question may wait on recipients who do not answer. When a question is the user's, whoever reads it asks the user by hand under a key of its own, and nothing stops two answerers from retrying one question, or a late answer from starting a stage that has moved on.

## Considered options

1. Each caller collects for itself, as today: it polls status or names a recipient it collects as, and flume changes nothing.
2. flume refuses a launch that names no recipient and runs in no session, and changes nothing else.
3. An address is a role of a stream. Every main-level role answers as a Flume kind at its own address, declared once by the source whose kinds run it and launched and ended by flume. A question goes to the role whose it is, of any stream, the asking agent naming it or the place's owner passing it. The stream's door, a role declared to stand behind it, stands behind every stall. A role says it is on a question with a hold whose bound lapses. An answer names the key of the stop-short it answers and is taken once. A recipient's start sees every place that waits on it. A question that is the user's is one card in the user's queue, raised by its caller's code on the word of the role whose question it is, its answer carried back by code.
4. flume carries a question that is the user's to a person's queue itself, through a seam of its own.
5. A standing watcher reads every place's status and carries questions to whoever answers, as the runner's copy into crew's records and the orchestrator's script do.
6. One answerer for a host, its planner, behind every place, and a session that serves an address standing in for a role that has not moved.
7. Option 3 with each role, and each stream's answerer, an entry of each host's configuration.
8. Option 3 with an address of a partition of its own, `<partition>/<role>`, beside the stream, as clause 132 has it.
9. Option 3 with a place's question put to the user by the role's run in the Inbox, in its own words, as its address.

### Context and problem statement

Record 0013's amendment lets a `[roles]` entry of the host's configuration give `agent`, the terminal's name for a standing agent, with an optional `session` and `stream`, in place of `kind`: the address's ends go into that agent and wake no run. Its crossing to a role that runs as a kind is that entry trading `agent` for `kind`. This record takes `[roles]` out of the host's configuration, so the standing entry needs a home, or an end.

### Decision outcome

**A source declares a standing agent as it declares a run.** An entry of a source's roles file may give `agent`, with an optional `session`, in place of `kind` and the run's keys, and may give `answers` and `answerer` beside it. Its stream is its address's, so it gives no `stream`: its agent is found in the session the host names for that stream, else the one it gives, in that stream's terminal (190). Its ends go into that agent as record 0013's amendment has them, and it wakes no run. An entry giving `agent` beside `kind` or a run's other keys, or beside `carried`, declares nothing for its address, and status names it, as every fault of a source's roles does.

So a role's crossing from a standing agent to its kind is its source's entry trading `agent` for `kind`, and nothing of the host's changes, as decision 13 of the change has every move. A standing operator may stand behind its stream before it moves, so the door stands from flume's release whether or not the operator runs as its kind yet. A standing planner or design agent is listed in every prompt with its words, and a stop-short naming it reaches its pane.

**Not taken:**

- **The standing entry kept in the host's `[roles]` while a run's entry moves to the source.** Two declarers of one source's roles, and the host repeating crew's partitions, which the user refused: "they are an artifact of crew".
- **Standing roles dropped, their questions reaching the door at the stall.** The user approved C.5's standing row the same day for the time before each move, and a question to a standing planner would wait out the stall bound for nothing.
