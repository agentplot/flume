## Context and problem statement

A stage's agent that cannot go on runs `flume needs`, and its stage ends short (41). The end goes to the place's recipients, and the place waits on its caller's retry (42, 43, 130). On deneb on 2026-11-05, 34 of 37 places had no recipient at all. A launch from a shell with no terminal session takes none (7), and the host's orchestrator launches that way, so its places' ends went to no one. The only reader was the orchestrator's own script, polling `flume status`. While the script was not running, overnight from 2026-11-04 to 11-05, two changes waited about nine hours on questions nobody saw.

The runner's places name the address `madswan/planner`. Clause 132 gives an address as `<partition>/<role>`, and no clause says what a partition is: flume reads the address as a key of the host's `[roles]` table, names the run from the part after the slash, and reads the part before it for nothing, beside the streams that already divide a host's work (143). crew's main-level roles, the design agent, the planner, ops, the reviewer and the operator, all stand as Claude Code sessions in herdr panes, outside flume, so nothing runs at that address and seven ends of one place stand pending (132). crew designed and built each role as a Flume kind at its own address (`roles-run-as-flume-kinds`), and has moved none onto it. flume reads a role's run from the host's own configuration, one `[roles]` entry per address, so a host of crew's would repeat every partition's every role, though crew already derives every fact of each run from its partitions into its own role record. crew had the runner launch and end role runs, since flume names a role's run by the role alone, so two partitions' planners in one repository would be one place, and since a run flume launches never ends of itself. Every place crew launches names the planner, though a question may be the design agent's or ops's.

A place launched from a herdr pane has its ends delivered into that pane. A restarted agent there gets a one-line notice about a place it never saw, and flume's plugin does nothing at the start of a session flume did not start. Nothing bounds how long a question may wait on recipients who do not answer. When a question is the user's, whoever reads it asks the user by hand under a key of its own, and nothing stops two answerers from retrying one question, or a late answer from starting a stage that has moved on.

## Considered options

1. Each caller collects for itself, as today: it polls status or names a recipient it collects as, and flume changes nothing.
2. flume refuses a launch that names no recipient and runs in no session, and changes nothing else.
3. An address is a role of a stream. Every main-level role answers as a Flume kind at its own address, declared once by the source whose kinds run it and launched and ended by flume. A question goes to the role whose it is, of any stream, the asking agent naming it or the place's owner passing it. The stream's door, a role declared to stand behind it, stands behind every stall. A role says it is on a question with a hold whose bound lapses. An answer names the key of the stop-short it answers and is taken once. A recipient's start sees every place that waits on it. A question that is the user's is asked once in the user's queue, by the role whose question it is.
4. flume carries a question that is the user's to a person's queue itself, through a seam of its own.
5. A standing watcher reads every place's status and carries questions to whoever answers, as the runner's copy into crew's records and the orchestrator's script do.
6. One answerer for a host, its planner, behind every place, and a session that serves an address standing in for a role that has not moved.
7. Option 3 with each role, and each stream's answerer, an entry of each host's configuration.
8. Option 3 with an address of a partition of its own, `<partition>/<role>`, beside the stream, as clause 132 has it.

## Decision outcome

Option 3.

**An address is a role of a stream.** A role's address is `<stream>/<role>`: the role a source declares for a stream the host declares (143). `madswan/ops` is ops of the stream `madswan`. A role's run runs in its address's stream. An address whose stream the host does not declare declares nothing, unless its source carries it to another host. A stop-short names its own stream's role by the role alone, `flume needs --to ops`, and any other stream's by its whole address, `--to swancloud/ops`: the stream bounds where a place's agents run, never whom they ask. A launch's recipient and a wake write an address whole, since a session's name is a bare word too. Since planning is a label, each of crew's partitions runs its work in one stream, and its roles are that stream's. The rest of what 132 built stands: `flume wake <address>`, a run's agent taking its address as its session's name and collecting for it, and status's list of addresses with ends pending.

**Roles are declared once, by their source.** A source the host declares (191) may name `roles`, the file in which it declares the roles it answers, written whole by the source's own tool and read by flume as it stands, never written. crew writes its file from its partitions: each address `<stream>/<role>` of the partition's stream, with its run's kind, one of crew's own packages and locked by the host, its repository, its name, intent and settings, and the words of what the role answers; each partition's operator standing behind its stream; and each address whose stream runs on another host, carried. Ten partitions' streams are ten addresses of one package. The host's configuration holds no role and no answerer. What of the host remains is the host's own: crew declared as a source, which says whose kinds and roles run under its accounts and where they lie on it; the lock of crew's kinds, its trust in their content; and its streams, one per partition it runs, with their room, pools and `planning`'s reserve. A fault stays its own: a roles file that cannot be read declares nothing, an entry that cannot be read, or whose address names a stream the host does not declare and is not carried, declares nothing for its address, and an address or a stream two declarations claim is neither's, each named in status.

**Roles answer as Flume kinds.** flume launches a role's run from its source's entry, on an end pending for its address or a carrier's `flume wake` (132). The runner launches no role's run: it carries crew's own events to flume by `flume wake`. A run is named by its entry's `name`, else `<stream>-<role>`, so two streams' runs of one role are two places, and runs in its address's stream, under its kind's label, with its intent and settings. A run is over once its turn delivers: flume declares it done and tidies it, with no caller's word. An address whose run is on another host is carried there by crew's records.

**A question goes to the role whose it is.** The launch names the place's owner: the planner for a bolt's work, ops for a fix of main, a role for its own landing work. The asking agent may address one question to another role of any stream, `flume needs --to <role or address>`, and the stop-short part of every prompt lists the roles with the words their source gives for what each answers, the place's own stream's by the role alone and every other stream's by its address. The question's end then goes to that role alone. A role that takes a question not its own passes it to the role whose it is. flume reads no question's words and picks no role.

**The door, and the bound.** A role a source declares may stand behind its address's stream, the stream's answerer: crew's operator of each partition, its door, behind the partition's stream. A launch that names no recipient and runs in no session takes it. A place waiting on its caller under no hold's words for its stall bound, five minutes unless the launch sets another, is stalled (130), and its stall goes to its owner, to the role its question named and to the door of the place's own stream, with the question's words and key. A role that is on a question says so with a hold on the waiting move, `flume hold <place> "<words>" --waiting --for <time>`, and the place does not stall. Past the hold's bound it lapses, and the place stalls again. A launch with no recipient and no session into a stream no role stands behind is refused, and so is one naming an address that wakes nothing where no answerer stands behind its stream.

**An answer is taken once.** Every end has a key, and every notice and every collected end gives it. A retry may name the key of the stop-short it answers, `--answers <key>`. It is then refused, naming who answered and when, once the place no longer waits on that stop-short.

**What a restarted recipient sees.** `flume inbox` gives every end pending for the recipient, and every place that waits on its caller and that the recipient is a recipient of, was asked by name, or was given a stall of, delivered or not, with the move, since when, the question's words, key and addressee, and a hold's words. Reading that list marks nothing. flume's Claude Code plugin's SessionStart hook, in a session flume did not start, collects as that session and gives the agent what the inbox gives. A role's run collects for its address at its start. A notice names the place, the first line of its brief, the end and its key.

**The user's question.** Which questions are the user's, and how one reaches the user, are the roles', never flume's (69, 72). A role's run reads flume's list for its address at its start. The user's queue is the Plannotator Inbox. A role holds the waiting move, then asks the user once, in its address's thread, as an Inbox identity of its address's that outlives each run, under a key made from the place and the end's key, so no second role or run asks again. The user's answer waits in the Inbox for that identity. crew's follower reads each role's identity at its minute and writes an ask of the role for a thread the user answered, the runner wakes the role by `flume wake`, and the next run reads the thread and retries with the key and the user's words. The door, at a stall no role took up, passes the question once to the role whose it is, or asks the user once itself. Plannotator 0.28.48 offers no watch and no identity for an address: the follower reads at its minute, and a run writes as its address by dropping its session's own variable; both are asks of Plannotator.

**No session serves an address.** Once roles run as kinds, a role the user opens to talk is a run, reached at its address, and a person's own session is reached as a session. Serving would reach only standing roles, which each partition's moves retire.

**Why not the others.** Option 1 is the nine hours: a question waits on a poller that may not be running, and record 0009 already refused polling for its load. Option 2 makes every shell launch name a recipient, but leaves an address that wakes nothing, a recipient that never answers and a restarted session where they are. Option 4 has flume tell a person something by itself (72), name a person's queue in its core (core.1) and judge which questions are a person's, which is its callers' (69). Option 5 is a second reader of flume's ends beside flume's own delivery, lagging by the watcher's pass and losing everything while it is down. Option 6 is the user's refusal: an agent may need something of ops or of the design agent apart from the planner, and a session serving an address is a fix beside crew's design rather than that design carried out. Option 7 is the user's refusal too: each host would repeat every partition's every role, rendered from crew's partitions by whoever writes the host's configuration, though the roles are crew's. Option 8 keeps a second division of the host's work beside the streams, which flume cannot check and every source and host repeats, and which the user questioned: "We're already going to use streams to partition where agents run."
