## Context and problem statement

A stage's agent that cannot go on runs `flume needs`, and its stage ends short (41). The end goes to the place's recipients, and the place waits on its caller's retry (42, 43, 130). On deneb on 2026-11-05, 34 of 37 places had no recipient at all. A launch from a shell with no terminal session takes none (7), and the host's orchestrator launches that way, so its places' ends went to no one. The only reader was the orchestrator's own script, polling `flume status`. While the script was not running, overnight from 2026-11-04 to 11-05, two changes waited about nine hours on questions nobody saw.

The runner's places name the address `madswan/planner`. crew's main-level roles, the design agent, the planner, ops, the reviewer and the operator, all stand as Claude Code sessions in herdr panes, outside flume, so nothing runs at that address and seven ends of one place stand pending (132). crew designed and built each role as a Flume kind at its own address (`roles-run-as-flume-kinds`), and has moved none onto it. It had the runner launch and end role runs, since flume names a role's run by the role alone, so two partitions' planners in one repository would be one place, and since a run flume launches never ends of itself. Every place crew launches names the planner, though a question may be the design agent's or ops's.

A place launched from a herdr pane has its ends delivered into that pane. A restarted agent there gets a one-line notice about a place it never saw, and flume's plugin does nothing at the start of a session flume did not start. Nothing bounds how long a question may wait on recipients who do not answer. When a question is the user's, whoever reads it makes a card by hand under a key of its own, and nothing stops two answerers from retrying one question, or a late answer from starting a stage that has moved on.

## Considered options

1. Each caller collects for itself, as today: it polls status or names a recipient it collects as, and flume changes nothing.
2. flume refuses a launch that names no recipient and runs in no session, and changes nothing else.
3. Every main-level role answers as a Flume kind at its own address, launched and ended by flume. A question goes to the role whose it is, the asking agent naming it or the place's owner passing it. The partition's door, the host's or a stream's answerer, stands behind every stall. A role says it is on a question with a hold whose bound lapses. An answer names the key of the stop-short it answers and is taken once. A recipient's start sees every place that waits on it. A question that is the user's becomes one card, made by the role whose question it is.
4. flume carries a question that is the user's to a person's queue itself, through a seam of its own.
5. A standing watcher reads every place's status and carries questions to whoever answers, as the runner's copy into crew's records and the orchestrator's script do.
6. One answerer for a host, its planner, behind every place, and a session that serves an address standing in for a role that has not moved.

## Decision outcome

Option 3.

**Roles answer as Flume kinds.** Each main-level role answers at its address, `<partition>/<role>`, as the kind crew's release carries for it. flume launches a role's run from the host's `[roles]` entry for its address, on an end pending for it or a carrier's `flume wake` (132). The runner launches no role's run: it carries crew's own events to flume by `flume wake`. The entry names the run's place, else `<partition>-<role>`, so two partitions' runs of one role are two places, and gives the run's intent and its agents' settings, as the runner's launch did. A run is over once its turn delivers: flume declares it done and tidies it, with no caller's word. An address whose run is on another host is carried there by crew's records.

**A question goes to the role whose it is.** The launch names the place's owner: the planner for a bolt's work, ops for a fix of main, a role for its own landing work. The asking agent may address one question to another role of the place's partition, `flume needs --to <address>`, and the stop-short part of every prompt lists those roles with the words the host's configuration gives for what each answers. The question's end then goes to that role alone. A role that takes a question not its own passes it to the role whose it is. flume reads no question's words and picks no role.

**The door, and the bound.** The host's configuration names the answerer behind its places, `[host] answerer` and a stream's own, a role's address it runs: on a host of crew's partitions, each partition's operator, its door. A launch that names no recipient and runs in no session takes it. A place waiting on its caller under no hold's words for its stall bound, five minutes unless the launch sets another, is stalled (130), and its stall goes to its owner, to the role its question named and to the door, with the question's words and key. A role that is on a question says so with a hold on the waiting move, `flume hold <place> "<words>" --waiting --for <time>`, and the place does not stall. Past the hold's bound it lapses, and the place stalls again. A launch with no recipient and no session where no answerer stands is refused, and so is one naming an address that wakes nothing where no answerer stands behind it.

**An answer is taken once.** Every end has a key, and every notice and every collected end gives it. A retry may name the key of the stop-short it answers, `--answers <key>`. It is then refused, naming who answered and when, once the place no longer waits on that stop-short.

**What a restarted recipient sees.** `flume inbox` gives every end pending for the recipient, and every place that waits on its caller and that the recipient is a recipient of, was asked by name, or was given a stall of, delivered or not, with the move, since when, the question's words, key and addressee, and a hold's words. Reading that list marks nothing. flume's Claude Code plugin's SessionStart hook, in a session flume did not start, collects as that session and gives the agent what the inbox gives. A role's run collects for its address at its start. A notice names the place, the first line of its brief, the end and its key.

**The user's question.** Which questions are the user's, and how one reaches the user, are the roles', never flume's (69, 72). A role holds the waiting move with words naming its card, then makes one card in its own name under a key made from the place and the end's key, so no second role or run makes another. The card's answer reaches the role's next run through crew's follower and the runner's `flume wake`, and the run retries with the key and the user's words. The door, at a stall no role took up, passes the question once to the role whose it is, or puts it to the user on that one card.

**No session serves an address.** Once roles run as kinds, a role the user opens to talk is a run, reached at its address, and a person's own session is reached as a session. Serving would reach only standing roles, which each partition's moves retire.

**Why not the others.** Option 1 is the nine hours: a question waits on a poller that may not be running, and record 0009 already refused polling for its load. Option 2 makes every shell launch name a recipient, but leaves an address that wakes nothing, a recipient that never answers and a restarted session where they are. Option 4 has flume tell a person something by itself (72), name a person's queue in its core (core.1) and judge which questions are a person's, which is its callers' (69). Option 5 is a second reader of flume's ends beside flume's own delivery, lagging by the watcher's pass and losing everything while it is down. Option 6 is the user's refusal: an agent may need something of ops or of the design agent apart from the planner, and a session serving an address is a fix beside crew's design rather than that design carried out.
