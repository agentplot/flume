# Every question a place asks reaches an answerer, the host's answerer behind its recipients, and an answer is taken once

Status: proposed, for the user's yes on the design of the change `every-question-a-place-asks-reaches-someone-who-answers-it`. Nothing here binds until then.

Deciders: the user, in the brief of that change on 2026-11-05: "Agents doing work in Flume are using Flume needs but I don't know that anything is paying attention to those Flume needs calls." The brief asks who answers for each kind of launch, what happens when no one collects an end within a bound, how a question that is the user's becomes a card answered once and never asked twice, with the answer going back to the place as its retry's words, and what a restarted recipient sees. The analysis and the shape are the constructing agent's, put to the user in the change's design.

## Context and problem statement

A stage's agent that cannot go on runs `flume needs`, and its stage ends short (41). The end goes to the place's recipients, and the place waits on its caller's retry (42, 43, 130). On deneb on 2026-11-05, 34 of 37 places had no recipient at all. A launch from a shell with no terminal session takes none (7), and the host's orchestrator launches that way, so its places' ends went to no one. The only reader was the orchestrator's own script, polling `flume status`. While the script was not running, overnight from 2026-11-04 to 11-05, two changes waited about nine hours on questions nobody saw.

The runner's places name the address `madswan/planner`. The host's configuration names no run for it, so nothing is ever woken for it, and seven ends of one place stand pending (132). None of the seven is a question. The runner never collects ends: it copies `flume status` into crew's records, crew's board shows a stop-short as "asks at <stage>", and the planner, a standing pane, sees it only when it reads the board. crew set flume's wake aside: flume names a role's run by the role alone, so two partitions' planners in one repository would be one place (`roles-run-as-flume-kinds`, Decision 1). The runner launches role runs of its own from crew's records instead.

A place launched from a herdr pane has its ends delivered into that pane. A restarted agent there gets a one-line notice about a place it never saw. An end delivered just before the restart is never shown again, and flume's plugin does nothing at the start of a session flume did not start. Nothing bounds how long a question may wait on recipients who do not answer. When a question is the user's, whoever reads it makes a card by hand under a key of its own, and nothing stops two answerers from retrying one question, or a late answer from starting a stage that has moved on.

## Considered options

1. Each caller collects for itself, as today: it polls status or names a recipient it collects as, and flume changes nothing.
2. flume refuses a launch that names no recipient and runs in no session, and changes nothing else.
3. The host's configuration names its answerer, a role's address. The answerer is the recipient of a launch that would have none, and every stall reaches it as well as the place's recipients. An address must have a run the host can wake, or a session that serves it. A role's run is named by its whole address. A caller holds the move a place waits on to say it is making it. An answer names the key of the stop-short it answers and is taken once. A recipient's start sees every place that waits on it. A question that is the user's becomes one card, made by the answerer's run under the end's key.
4. flume carries a question that is the user's to a person's queue itself, through a seam of its own.
5. A standing watcher reads every place's status and carries questions to whoever answers, as the runner's copy into crew's records and the orchestrator's script do.

## Decision outcome

Option 3.

**Who answers.** A place's recipients are the launch's. Where the launch names none, they are `$FLUME_RECIPIENT`, else the launching session as the terminal names it, else the host's answerer: the address the configuration names for the place's stream, or for the host. A launch from a shell on a host whose configuration names no answerer, and that names no recipient, is refused, saying how to name one. So is a launch naming an address that the configuration names no run for and that no session serves. Every place then has a recipient that collects, or one that a run is woken for.

**A session may serve an address.** A session that says it serves an address, by `flume serve <address>`, is delivered that address's ends while it is live. No run is woken for an end it can be delivered. A standing role's session serves its role's address, so a role that stands and a role that runs as a kind are reached alike. A role's run is named by the whole address, `<kind>--<partition>-<role>`, so two partitions' runs of one role never share a place. That removes the reason crew gave for launching role runs through the runner. flume becomes the one launcher of a role's run. The runner, and crew's follower of card answers, carry their own events to it by `flume wake <address>` and launch no role's run themselves. crew's change says the user chose the runner as that launcher, so this part is theirs to rule on its own.

**The bound.** A place waiting on its caller under no hold's words for its stall bound, five minutes unless the launch sets another, is stalled (130). Its stall is delivered to each recipient, as today, and to the host's answerer where the answerer is not one of them. The stall carries the words and the key of the stop-short the place waits on, so a reader who never saw the place has the question. A recipient that is making the move says so with a hold on it, `flume hold <place> --waiting "<words>"`, and may give the hold a bound, after which it lapses and the place stalls. A place whose recipient is handling its question is then neither stalled nor passed to the answerer.

**An answer is taken once.** Every end has a key, and every notice and every collected end gives it. A retry may name the key of the stop-short it answers, `--answers <key>`. It is then refused, naming who answered and when, once the place no longer waits on that stop-short. Two answerers can no longer both restart a stage, and a late answer to a card starts nothing. Within the open session's hour, the open-session record's answer is the same retry and is guarded the same way.

**What a restarted recipient sees.** `flume inbox` gives every end pending for the recipient. It also gives every place the recipient is a recipient of, or the host's answerer behind, that waits on its caller, delivered or not, with the move, since when, the stop-short's words and key, and a hold's words. Reading that list marks nothing. flume's Claude Code plugin's SessionStart hook, in a session flume did not start, collects for that session and for the address it serves, and gives the agent what the inbox gives, or nothing where nothing is there. A notice names the place, the first line of its brief, the end and its key, and the command that reads it whole.

**The user's question.** Which questions are the user's, and how one reaches the user, are the answerer's, never flume's (69, 72). The answerer's run follows one protocol, which crew's planner and any host's own answering kind keep. It answers from what it can read, by the retry with the key. A question it cannot answer is the user's: it holds the waiting move with words naming its card, and makes one card in the user's queue under a key made from the place and the end's key, so neither a second run nor a second answerer makes another. When the user answers, the queue's carrier runs `flume wake <address>`. The woken run reads the answer and retries with the key and the user's words, and the fresh agent's prompt carries the question with the answer (42, 49). A card whose question no longer waits is withdrawn by the run that next reads it.

**Why not the others.** Option 1 is the nine hours: a question waits on a poller that may not be running, and record 0009 already refused polling for its load. Option 2 makes every shell launch name a recipient, but leaves an address that wakes nothing, a recipient that never answers and a restarted session where they are. Option 4 has flume tell a person something by itself (72), name a person's queue in its core (core.1) and judge which questions are a person's, which is its callers' (69). Option 5 is a second reader of flume's ends beside flume's own delivery. It lags by the watcher's pass, loses everything while the watcher is down, and needs a standing process, which the family refuses.
